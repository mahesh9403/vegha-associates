What this calculator computes

A home loan EMI calculator turns three inputs into the one number you will live with for the next two decades. You supply the principal you intend to borrow, the annual rate of interest your lender has quoted and the tenure in years. The tool returns your equated monthly instalment, the total interest payable over the full tenure, the total amount repaid (principal plus interest) and a complete amortisation schedule showing how every instalment splits between interest and principal repayment.

The formula in words

An EMI is the single fixed payment that clears both interest and principal by the end of the tenure, with interest charged on the reducing balance. Convert the annual rate into a monthly rate by dividing by twelve and by a hundred, convert the tenure into a number of months, then compound the monthly rate over that count.

EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)

Here P is the loan principal, r is the monthly rate of interest as a decimal (the annual rate divided by 1200) and n is the total number of monthly instalments (years multiplied by twelve).

How to read the results

  • The EMI figure is your committed monthly cash outflow. Most lenders in India assess your eligibility on total obligations staying within a comfortable share of net monthly income, so treat this number as a budgeting input, not just an output.
  • Total interest tells you the true cost of the borrowing. On a long tenure it can approach or exceed the principal itself, which is why the same EMI over a shorter tenure is almost always the cheaper choice.
  • The amortisation schedule shows a heavy interest bias in the early years. In the first third of the tenure most of each instalment services interest, and only later does principal repayment accelerate.

Practical points for Indian borrowers

  • Most floating rate housing loans are linked to an external benchmark, usually the repo rate. When the benchmark moves, lenders typically hold the EMI steady and adjust the tenure instead, so ask your bank which lever they will pull and get the revised schedule in writing.
  • The EMI does not include processing fees, legal and valuation charges, stamp duty, registration or property insurance. Budget for these separately, since they are paid upfront from your own funds.
  • Under the old tax regime, interest on a self occupied house property qualifies for deduction under section 24(b) and the principal component counts towards section 80C, both within the limits prescribed in the Act. The amortisation schedule is exactly what your assessing officer or auditor will want to see for the split.

Answers

Frequently asked questions

Does a longer tenure reduce my home loan cost?

No. A longer tenure reduces the monthly instalment but increases the total interest you pay, often substantially. Stretching a loan from fifteen years to twenty five years can add several lakh rupees of interest on the same principal. Choose the shortest tenure your monthly cash flow can comfortably absorb.

Will my EMI change if the repo rate changes?

On a floating rate loan the effective interest cost changes at every reset date. Most Indian lenders keep the EMI constant and lengthen or shorten the tenure instead, though you can usually request an EMI revision. On a fixed rate loan the instalment stays unchanged for the fixed period agreed in the sanction letter.

What is pre EMI interest and does this calculator include it?

Pre EMI is the interest only payment charged on the portion of the loan disbursed while a property is still under construction. This calculator assumes full disbursement at the start, so the regular EMI schedule begins immediately. For a stage wise disbursement, run the calculation on each tranche or ask us to model the full drawdown.

How much home loan can I actually get sanctioned?

Lenders look at two limits together: the loan to value ratio against the property value, and your repayment capacity based on income and existing obligations. The sanctioned amount is the lower of the two. This calculator prices a loan you specify, it does not assess eligibility, so treat the output as a planning estimate.
