What this calculator computes

This tool takes your existing home loan (outstanding principal, current rate of interest and the number of instalments still remaining) and adds a prepayment on top: either a one time lump sum, or a fixed extra amount paid with every EMI, or both. It then rebuilds the amortisation schedule and reports the revised tenure, the number of months saved, the interest you would have paid without the prepayment, the interest you will now pay and the net saving.

The formula in words

A prepayment does not change the EMI or the rate. It reduces the outstanding principal immediately, so every subsequent month accrues interest on a smaller base and a larger slice of each instalment goes towards principal. The loan therefore closes early. With the EMI held constant, the remaining number of instalments on a balance is given by the following relationship, where P is the outstanding principal after the prepayment and r is the monthly rate as a decimal.

n = log(EMI / (EMI - (P x r))) / log(1 + r)

Interest saved is simply the total interest under the original schedule minus the total interest under the rebuilt schedule.

How to read the results

  • Timing dominates size. A prepayment made in the early years of a loan saves far more than the same rupee amount paid in the final years, because early instalments are mostly interest.
  • The extra monthly option is usually the more powerful habit. Adding even a modest amount to every EMI compounds into a large tenure cut over time, and it is easier to sustain than saving up for a lump sum.
  • Compare the interest saved against what the same money would earn elsewhere after tax. A prepayment is a guaranteed, tax free return equal to your loan rate.

Practical points for Indian borrowers

  • On floating rate housing loans taken by individual borrowers, lenders are not permitted to levy foreclosure or prepayment charges. Fixed rate loans can attract a charge, so read the sanction letter before you pay.
  • Ask the bank in writing whether the prepayment should reduce the tenure or the EMI. Reducing the tenure saves more interest; reducing the EMI improves monthly cash flow. The default at many banks is not the one you want.
  • If you claim deduction on housing loan interest under the old regime, a prepayment reduces that deduction in later years. Weigh the interest saved against the tax benefit forgone, and collect a revised amortisation statement after every prepayment.

Answers

Frequently asked questions

Can my bank charge me a penalty for prepaying my home loan?

For floating rate home loans sanctioned to individual borrowers, the Reserve Bank of India does not permit lenders to levy foreclosure charges or prepayment penalties. Fixed rate loans and loans to non individual borrowers can attract a charge as per the sanction terms. Always check the specific clause in your loan agreement before making a large payment.

Should I reduce the tenure or reduce the EMI after a prepayment?

Reducing the tenure while keeping the EMI unchanged saves considerably more interest, because you keep paying the same amount against a smaller balance. Reducing the EMI frees up monthly cash flow but leaves the loan running for its original term. If your income is stable, keep the EMI and cut the tenure.

Is prepaying better than investing the same money?

Prepayment gives you a certain, risk free saving equal to your loan rate of interest, with no tax on that saving. An investment must beat that rate after tax and after allowing for risk to be the better choice. Also consider the interest deduction you may be claiming under the old tax regime, which lowers your effective cost of borrowing.

How often can I make partial prepayments?

Most Indian lenders allow partial prepayments at any time through net banking or at the branch, sometimes with a minimum amount such as one EMI. There is generally no cap on frequency for floating rate retail home loans. Ask for an updated repayment schedule after each payment so your records and your tax certificate stay accurate.
