What this calculator computes

India offers individual taxpayers two parallel ways of computing tax on the same income. The new regime applies lower slab rates but withdraws most deductions and exemptions. The old regime keeps those deductions but taxes at higher slab rates. This calculator computes the liability under both, using the rates notified for the assessment year selected, and presents the two figures side by side along with the difference.

How the comparison works

Both computations start from the same gross total income: salary, income from house property, business or professional income, capital gains and income from other sources. From there the paths separate.

  • Under the old regime, the calculator subtracts the exemptions and chapter VI A deductions you claim, arrives at total income, applies the old slab rates, then applies rebate, surcharge where applicable and health and education cess.
  • Under the new regime, most of those deductions are ignored. The calculator applies the new slab rates to the resulting total income, then applies the rebate available under that regime, surcharge and cess.
  • The lower of the two figures is the regime that suits you for that year, and the calculator shows the saving in rupees.

Tax payable = (slab tax on total income) less rebate, plus surcharge if applicable, plus health and education cess on the total of tax and surcharge

Which deductions matter in the old regime

  • Section 80C investments and payments such as provident fund, life insurance premium, principal repayment on a housing loan, tax saving deposits and equity linked savings schemes, within the overall ceiling.
  • Health insurance premium under section 80D, interest on housing loan under section 24(b), interest on education loan under section 80E, contribution to the national pension system, and donations under section 80G.
  • Salary linked exemptions including house rent allowance and leave travel concession, which are not available under the new regime.

Practical points

  • Slab rates, rebate thresholds and even the standard deduction are revised in the annual Finance Act. Always select the correct assessment year, and do not carry last year conclusion into this year without rerunning the numbers.
  • The new regime is the default. If the old regime suits you better, you must exercise the option in the manner and within the time prescribed, and taxpayers with business income face restrictions on switching back and forth.
  • Broadly, the more genuine deductions you have (rent paid with HRA, a housing loan, insurance, retirement contributions), the more likely the old regime wins. With few deductions, the new regime usually wins. The only way to be sure is to compute both, which is precisely what this tool does.

Answers

Frequently asked questions

Which regime should I choose?

There is no universal answer, because it depends entirely on the deductions you can genuinely claim. Taxpayers paying substantial rent, servicing a housing loan and making full use of section 80C and 80D often do better under the old regime, while those with few deductions usually pay less under the new one. Compute both for your actual figures each year and choose the lower.

Can I switch between the old and new regime every year?

A salaried taxpayer without business income can generally choose afresh each year while filing the return, irrespective of what was declared to the employer. A taxpayer with income from business or profession faces restrictions: the option to move out of the new regime can normally be exercised only once, after which returning is limited. The new regime is the default if no option is exercised.

Why does the calculator not show slab rates in the text?

Slab rates, rebate limits and thresholds are amended by the Finance Act almost every year, so printing them in the page copy would go stale quickly and could mislead. The calculator itself applies the rates notified for the assessment year you select. For a position specific to your facts, speak to us before you file.

Does this calculator handle capital gains and business income?

Special rate incomes such as certain capital gains are taxed at their own prescribed rates and are not simply added to slab income, so they need to be entered separately where the tool provides for it. Business and professional income also brings in presumptive taxation options, depreciation and advance tax obligations. For anything beyond straightforward salary and interest income, treat the output as indicative and have the computation reviewed.
