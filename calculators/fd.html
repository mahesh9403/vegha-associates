What this calculator computes

A fixed deposit pays a contracted rate of interest on a lump sum for a fixed term. This calculator takes the principal, the annual rate of interest offered and the tenure, and returns the maturity value and the total interest earned. The default assumes quarterly compounding, which is the convention followed by most Indian banks on cumulative deposits.

The formula in words

Interest is credited to the deposit every quarter and then itself earns interest for the remaining quarters. Divide the annual rate by four to get the quarterly rate, and raise it to the power of the number of quarters in the tenure.

Maturity value = P x (1 + (r / 4))^(4 x t)

P is the principal, r is the annual rate of interest as a decimal and t is the tenure in years. Interest earned is the maturity value minus the principal.

Cumulative versus payout deposits

  • In a cumulative deposit, interest stays in the account, compounds every quarter and is paid out along with the principal on maturity. This gives the highest maturity value and is what the formula above computes.
  • In a payout or non cumulative deposit, interest is paid to your bank account monthly, quarterly, half yearly or annually. Nothing compounds, so the total interest is lower, but you get a regular income stream. Monthly payout options are usually offered at a slightly discounted rate.
  • Choose payout if you need the cash flow, for example a retired parent living on interest income. Choose cumulative if you do not, since compounding does the work.

Practical points for Indian depositors

  • Interest on a fixed deposit is fully taxable as income from other sources at your slab rate, and it is taxable on accrual each year even in a cumulative deposit where you receive nothing until maturity. Banks deduct tax at source once interest for the year crosses the threshold specified in the Act, and you can file Form 15G or Form 15H if your total income is below the taxable limit.
  • Breaking a deposit early usually attracts a penalty and re pricing at the rate applicable for the period actually run. Laddering across several smaller deposits with staggered maturities gives you liquidity without breaking everything at once.
  • Deposits with scheduled banks are insured by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation up to the prescribed limit per depositor per bank. Senior citizens are typically offered an additional rate over the card rate, and a five year tax saving deposit qualifies for deduction under section 80C in the old regime, subject to a lock in.

Answers

Frequently asked questions

Why does my bank show a different maturity value?

Differences usually come down to the compounding convention and day count. Most banks compound quarterly on cumulative deposits, but some use a different basis for tenures under six months, where simple interest is applied. Rounding and the exact number of days in each quarter can also shift the figure slightly, so treat this output as an accurate estimate and the bank advice as final.

Is FD interest taxable if I have not received it yet?

Yes. Interest on a cumulative fixed deposit is generally taxable on accrual, year by year, even though you receive the money only at maturity. Banks report it and deduct tax at source once the annual interest crosses the threshold in the Act. Declaring it annually avoids a large and unexpected liability in the year of maturity.

What happens if I break my FD before maturity?

The bank typically reworks the interest to the rate that was applicable for the period the deposit actually ran, and may apply a premature withdrawal penalty on top. You therefore earn less than the contracted rate. Some banks offer deposits with no premature penalty at a marginally lower rate, which is worth asking about if you may need the money.

Does a tax saving FD work differently?

A five year tax saving fixed deposit qualifies for deduction under section 80C for taxpayers under the old regime, within the overall section 80C limit. In exchange it carries a mandatory lock in for the full five years, cannot be broken prematurely and cannot be pledged as security for a loan. The interest earned remains fully taxable.
