What this calculator computes

The Public Provident Fund is a government backed small savings scheme with a fifteen year term. This calculator takes your annual contribution, the applicable rate of interest and the number of years, and returns the maturity value, the total you will have contributed and the total interest credited. The default term is fifteen years, and you can extend the calculation in blocks of five years to model an extension.

The formula in words

Each year contribution earns interest that is credited to the account at the end of the financial year and then earns interest itself in every later year. The result is the future value of a series of annual deposits.

Maturity value = A x (((1 + r)^n - 1) / r) x (1 + r)

A is the annual contribution, r is the rate of interest as a decimal and n is the number of years. Note that within a year, interest is computed on the lowest balance in the account between the close of the fifth day and the last day of each month, so the timing of your deposit matters.

How to read the results

  • Watch how the interest component builds. In the early years contributions dominate, but by the closing years of the fifteen year term the annual interest credit alone can exceed your annual deposit.
  • The rate is not fixed for the life of the account. The government notifies the PPF rate every quarter, so the actual maturity value will differ from any single rate projection. Run the calculation at a slightly conservative rate.
  • Contributions must fall within the minimum and maximum annual limits prescribed for the scheme. Amounts deposited beyond the ceiling earn no interest and are simply returned.

Practical points for Indian savers

  • Deposit before the fifth of the month, and ideally in the first week of April for the full year, so that the entire amount qualifies for interest for that month and that year.
  • PPF enjoys exempt exempt exempt treatment: the contribution is eligible for deduction under section 80C in the old regime, the interest credited is exempt, and the maturity proceeds are exempt. This makes the effective post tax return attractive against a taxable fixed deposit at a similar headline rate.
  • Partial withdrawals and loans against the balance are permitted from the years specified in the scheme rules, and the account can be extended after maturity in blocks of five years, either with or without further contributions. The balance is also protected from attachment by a court decree in respect of debts.

Answers

Frequently asked questions

What rate of interest does PPF pay?

The rate is notified by the Ministry of Finance every quarter as part of the small savings rate review, and it applies to all PPF accounts for that quarter. It has changed several times over the years, so no single rate holds for a full fifteen year term. Check the current notification before relying on a projection.

Can I withdraw from PPF before fifteen years?

Partial withdrawal is permitted from the year specified in the scheme rules, subject to a cap linked to the balance in earlier years. A loan facility is also available in the early years at a small spread over the prevailing rate. Premature closure is allowed only in limited situations such as specified medical treatment or higher education, and it carries an interest penalty.

Is PPF interest taxable?

No. PPF falls under the exempt exempt exempt category, so the interest credited each year and the amount received on maturity are both exempt from income tax. The contribution itself qualifies for deduction under section 80C, but only if you are taxed under the old regime. Under the new regime you still get the tax free interest and maturity, just not the deduction.

What happens when the fifteen year term ends?

You may withdraw the entire balance tax free, or extend the account in blocks of five years. An extension can be with fresh contributions, in which case you must submit the prescribed form within a year of maturity, or without contributions, in which case the balance keeps earning interest and you may withdraw once a year. Choosing not to notify the bank or post office defaults you to the second option.
